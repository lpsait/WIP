Overview
Qualification Summary
⚠️
Non-Negotiable — Assessment Complete by 13 February 2026
Every unit must be assessed, signed, and in the IQA queue by 13 February 2026. This is not a suggested target — it is the required internal deadline. The scheme of work below has been built backwards from this date. Follow it week by week without skipping or deferring units.
Assessment Model
Observation + SAQ — Every Unit
Each of the 10 units requires a practical observation recording form AND a short answer questions form. Both must be completed, signed, and dated on the day before the unit is considered done.
Grading
Pass Only
No merit or distinction grading at E3. Pass is the only outcome. If a learner fails any component, a targeted resubmission must be issued within 5 working days — not left unresolved.
Pace
1 Unit Every 2 Weeks
With 10 units and 23 teaching weeks before mid-February, the scheme runs at approximately one unit per fortnight. There is no slack built in. If a unit slips, it must be caught up in the same fortnight — not deferred.

03
Assessment Process
Steps to Follow — Every Unit, Every Learner
📋 Before Each Unit
  1. 1
    Confirm Learner Registration on Walled Garden
    Every learner must be registered for the 3902 qualification before any assessment. Evidence from unregistered learners cannot be claimed.
  2. 2
    Print Observation and SAQ Forms
    Print both the Assessor Observation Recording Form and Short Answer Questions Recording Form for the specific unit. Have them ready before the session — not during it.
  3. 3
    Brief the Learner on the Criteria
    Explain exactly what the learner must demonstrate. Read the observation criteria out loud so there are no surprises during the task.
  4. 4
    Risk Assessment Signed
    For all practical units involving vehicle work, the relevant risk assessment must be signed by the learner before work begins. File the signed copy in the portfolio on the day.
🔧 During and After
  1. 1
    Observe — Do Not Assist
    Do not guide or correct during the assessed task. If a safety risk arises, stop the task and record it as a resubmission — not a pass.
  2. 2
    Write Specific Comments — No Blank Boxes
    Record exactly what the learner did for each criterion. "Completed the task" is not acceptable. Describe the action, the tool used, and the outcome observed.
  3. 3
    Both Sign Every Page — On the Day
    Assessor and learner sign each page on the day of assessment. Retrospective signing is rejected by IQA without exception.
  4. 4
    File and Submit to IQA Within One Week
    Evidence goes into the portfolio on the day. Unit tracker updated. Submitted to IQA within one week. Do not batch at year-end — this is what caused delays in previous years.

06
Post-Qualification Enrichment
March → June 2026 — Technical Skills Programme
🔧
What this programme is for
Once qualifications are complete, learners have 16 weeks of full workshop access before the end of the academic year. This programme uses that time to build technical skills that go beyond the E3 qualification — preparing learners for progression to Level 1 the following year, and giving them genuine hands-on experience that employers and the next level of study require. All activities are practical and workshop-based. No additional assessment forms are required — these are developmental, not certificated at E3 level.
March 2026 — Weeks 1–4
Engine Strip and Rebuild
Learners strip a complete engine down to major components — head, block, sump, valvetrain — and rebuild it. Focus on torque sequences, gasket replacement, and understanding what they identified in 3902-011 in a real working context. Suitable engines: small capacity petrol unit from a donor vehicle. Introduce engine reconditioning tools: torque wrench, feeler gauges, straight edge and plastigauge for bearing clearances.
EngineStrip & RebuildTorqueProgression to L1
April 2026 — Weeks 5–8
Braking System Service and Overhaul
Full brake overhaul on a workshop vehicle: front and rear pads, front discs, handbrake cable adjustment, and brake fluid bleed. Introduce brake pipe flaring using a flaring kit. Discuss hydraulic principles at a practical level — what happens when air enters the circuit and why bleeding order matters. Link to unit 3902-006 knowledge and extend it with real fault diagnosis: sticking calipers, uneven pad wear, spongy pedal causes.
BrakesFault DiagnosisHydraulicsOverhaul
April/May 2026 — Weeks 9–11
Introduction to Diagnostic Equipment
Introduce the OBD-II diagnostic scan tool. Learners connect to workshop vehicles, read live data (RPM, coolant temp, O2 sensor voltage, throttle position), identify and clear fault codes, and discuss what the codes mean. Introduce the concept of freeze frame data. Cross-reference readings to the systems covered in the E3 qual — this is where classroom knowledge becomes genuinely useful. Use a vehicle with seeded faults if possible.
DiagnosticsOBD-IILive DataFault Codes
May 2026 — Weeks 12–13
Electric and Hybrid Vehicle Awareness
Introduction to EV and hybrid drivetrain architecture — HV battery, inverter, motor, regenerative braking. Focus on safe working awareness: orange cable identification, HV isolation procedures, PPE for HV work. No hands-on HV work — awareness and identification only. Use cut-away diagrams, the ESC EV interactive app, and where possible, arrange for learners to observe a hybrid vehicle being safely isolated.
EV AwarenessHV SafetyHybrid SystemsIndustry Ready
May/June 2026 — Weeks 14–15
Motorsport Engineering Taster
Link the technical skills from the year to the motorsport context. Activities: corner weighting using corner weight scales, tyre pressure and stagger calculations, basic geometry checks (camber with an angle gauge), and an introduction to setup sheets. Use ESR CityCar Cup resources where available. Learners complete a simple setup sheet for a workshop vehicle. Discuss how road car servicing principles translate to competition car preparation.
MotorsportSetupCorner WeightsGeometry
June 2026 — Week 16
End of Year Showcase & Progression Planning
Final week: learners prepare a short verbal or visual presentation on one topic they covered during the enrichment programme — what they did, what they learned, what surprised them. Staff use this as evidence of progression for UCAS/applications. One-to-one session with each learner: review their E3 certificate, confirm progression route (Level 1 next year), and discuss any industry or apprenticeship interest. Employer visitor session if possible.
