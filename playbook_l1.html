01
Overview
5 Units — One Critical Constraint
⚠️
All units assessed and IQA-ready by 13 February 2026
Five units is a short qualification — there is no reason it should run to year-end. The scheme below completes all assessment by mid-February, leaving 16 weeks for meaningful technical enrichment. Follow the sequence without deviation.
📅
Unit 7290-031 — Start This in September
Unit 031 (Working Relationships Skills) requires 3 separate observed occasions on different dates, plus witness testimony from peers and a supervisor. It cannot be compressed into a single session. Start observing and recording from the first week — do not treat it as a standalone unit to be done later. By the time other units are complete, 031 should already have all 3 occasions recorded.

03
Assessment Process
Steps to Follow — Every Unit, Every Learner
📋 Before Each Unit
  1. 1
    Confirm Registration on Walled Garden
    All learners registered for 7290-10 before any assessment. Unregistered learner evidence cannot be claimed.
  2. 2
    Print the Correct Forms
    Print the observation and/or SAQ recording form for that specific unit number. Have them ready before the session.
  3. 3
    Brief the Learner on Criteria
    Read out the observation criteria before the task begins. Learners must know what they are being assessed against. For unit 051 — read the HASAWA statement before the H&S section.
  4. 4
    Risk Assessment Signed (702)
    For unit 702, the risk assessment and COSHH review must be signed by the learner before any practical work begins. Coolant COSHH datasheet must be available and discussed.
🔧 During and After
  1. 1
    Observe — Do Not Assist
    Do not guide or correct during the assessed task. Record what you observe. If a safety risk arises, stop and record as resubmission.
  2. 2
    Write Specific Comments — No Blank Boxes
    Describe exactly what the learner did for each criterion. For unit 031 — describe the specific conduct observed on each of the 3 occasions separately, including dates and context.
  3. 3
    Both Sign Every Page — On the Day
    Assessor and learner sign each page on the day. Retrospective signing is rejected without exception.
  4. 4
    File and Submit to IQA Within One Week
    Evidence into portfolio on the day. Unit tracker updated. IQA submission within one week. Never batch at year-end.

06
Post-Qualification Enrichment
March → June 2026 — Technical Skills Programme
🔧
Purpose of this programme
With only 5 units, the L1 qualification covers a narrow range of content. The enrichment programme uses the remaining 16 weeks to extend learners technically — particularly toward the L2 qualification content they will encounter the following year. All sessions are workshop-based with full vehicle access. No additional City & Guilds assessment is required for this phase.
March 2026 — Weeks 1–4
Routine Vehicle Service — L2 Preview
Introduce the full routine service procedure that L2 unit 101 requires. Learners carry out oil and filter change, battery voltage check, antifreeze concentration test, brake fluid level, and all visual checks. Introduce the OBD diagnostic reader — connect to a vehicle, read live data, identify and clear codes. This directly prepares learners for L2 delivery next year and gives them a significant practical confidence boost.
Routine ServiceL2 PreparationDiagnosticsOBD-II
April 2026 — Weeks 5–8
Braking and Chassis Systems
Extend the L1 knowledge into a full braking service: front pad and disc replacement, rear drum inspection, handbrake cable check, and a full brake fluid bleed. Introduce brake pipe inspection — corrosion identification, flare types. Move into chassis inspection: steering rack play, wheel bearing check, CV joint condition, and damper operation. Learners complete a simple vehicle condition sheet — directly relevant to the L2 pre-MOT inspection unit.
BrakesChassis InspectionPre-MOT SkillsL2 Preparation
April/May 2026 — Weeks 9–11
Fabrication and Materials
Introduce the L2 unit 004 fabrication skills: using a hacksaw, files, taps and dies, and measuring instruments (vernier caliper, micrometer). Learners make a simple engineering component to a drawing — not to L2 assessment standard but familiarising them with the process and tools. Cover material types: low carbon steel, cast iron, copper, ABS plastic. Introduce correct tool storage and workshop housekeeping to L2 standard.
FabricationMeasuring ToolsMaterialsL2 Unit 004 Prep
May 2026 — Weeks 12–13
Electric Vehicle and Hybrid Awareness
Industry context session: EV drivetrain architecture, HV battery systems, regenerative braking, and charging types. Focus on safe working awareness — orange cable identification, HV isolation procedures, PPE for HV environments. No hands-on HV work. Use the ESC EV interactive app and cut-away diagrams. Arrange observation of a hybrid vehicle isolation if possible. This topic appears in the L2 qualification and increasingly in employer expectations.
EV SystemsHV SafetyHybridIndustry Ready
May/June 2026 — Weeks 14–15
Motorsport Engineering Taster
Applied session linking road car servicing to motorsport preparation. Corner weighting using corner weight scales, tyre pressure and temperature management, camber measurement with an angle gauge. Learners complete a basic vehicle setup sheet. Introduce the CityCar Cup technical regulations and how the series vehicle differs from a road car. Use ESR resources where available. Gives learners a unique enrichment context not available elsewhere.
MotorsportSetupCorner WeightsCityCar Cup
June 2026 — Week 16
End of Year Showcase & L2 Readiness Review
Final week: learners present one topic from the enrichment programme — what they did, what they built, what they learned. Staff record this as evidence of progression for UCAS/applications. One-to-one progression meeting: review L1 certificate, confirm L2 enrolment for next year, identify any knowledge gaps to address over summer. Industry employer visitor session if possible — relevant to apprenticeship pathways.
