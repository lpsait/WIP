Overview
10 Units — 3 Evidence Types — One Deadline
⚠️
All units assessed and IQA-ready by 13 February 2026. Non-negotiable.
This qualification has taken too long to deliver in previous years. The scheme below is built backwards from mid-February. There is no flexibility in this timeline — if a unit looks like it will slip its target date, escalate to the Coordinator immediately, not at the next team meeting.
🖥
Units 151 and 155 — Evolve Online Tests — Book by December
Units 151 and 155 are assessed by online multiple-choice test via City & Guilds Evolve only — there is no observation form or SAQ pack. Tests must be booked via Walled Garden as soon as taught content is complete. The timeline targets unit 151 booked by end of November, sat in January; unit 155 booked by end of January, sat in February. Tests must be sat under JCQ invigilated conditions within the candidate's registration period — if registration lapses before the test is sat, the unit cannot be achieved.
📋
Unit 602 — Optional Unit — Obtain Documentation First
Unit 602 is not included in the standard assessment pack (7290-12/17 V1.0). Obtain documentation from City & Guilds via Walled Garden or your EQA before beginning delivery. Confirm rules of combination for your cohort with the Coordinator before deciding whether to offer this unit.

03
Assessment Process
Steps to Follow — Every Unit, Every Learner
📋 Before Every Assessment
  1. 1
    Confirm Registration
    Verify on Walled Garden before every unit — not just at the start of year. Check Diploma (7290-12) vs Certificate (7290-17) route for each learner.
  2. 2
    Print the Correct Forms
    Print the specific recording form for that unit. For 151 and 155: no form — book the Evolve test. Have forms ready before the session.
  3. 3
    Brief the Candidate on Criteria
    Read out the assessment criteria before the task begins. This is a City & Guilds requirement. For 051 — read the HASAWA statement before the H&S section.
  4. 4
    Complete Vehicle Information First (101, 105)
    Vehicle make/model, registration, and VIN recorded before any practical work begins. Forms without this are rejected.
🔧 During and After
  1. 1
    Observe — Do Not Assist
    No guidance or correction during assessment. Any assistance given must be noted. Safety risk: stop task, record as resubmission.
  2. 2
    Specific Comments and All Readings Attached
    Name what the learner did. Record all numerical readings (voltages, emissions, torques, tyre depths) on or attached to the form. No blank feedback boxes.
  3. 3
    Use Supplementary Oral Questions (Obs-Only Units)
    For units 004, 101, 105 — ask relevant questions from the 10 supplementary knowledge questions. Record question number and verbatim response on form.
  4. 4
    Sign, File, Submit to IQA Within One Week
    Both sign every page on the day. Evidence into portfolio same day. IQA submission within one week. Never batch.

06
Post-Qualification Enrichment
March → June 2026 — Advanced Technical Programme
🔧
Purpose of this programme
Having completed the L2 qualification by mid-February, learners have 16 weeks of full workshop time remaining. This programme moves beyond the qualification into genuinely advanced workshop skills — the kind that make learners competitive for Level 3 progression, apprenticeships, and employer selection. All sessions are practical and workshop-based. No additional City & Guilds assessment required.
March 2026 — Weeks 1–4
Advanced Diagnostics and Fault Finding
Move beyond basic code reading into live data analysis. Learners use a professional-grade scan tool (Snap-on, Autel or similar) to analyse multiple PIDs simultaneously on a running engine — coolant temp, IAT, MAF, O2 sensors, short/long term fuel trims, injector pulse width. Introduce freeze frame data analysis. Seed faults into workshop vehicles and challenge learners to diagnose without being told the fault. Introduce oscilloscope basics: reading a crank sensor waveform and an injector waveform.
Advanced DiagnosticsLive DataOscilloscopeFault Finding
April 2026 — Weeks 5–7
Engine Management and Fuel Systems
Deep dive into the engine management system: ECU inputs and outputs, sensor types (NTC thermistor, potentiometer, Hall effect), actuators (injectors, idle control valve, EGR). Learners use a multimeter and oscilloscope to test sensors on a live or bench engine. Introduce lambda (O2) sensor operation and how fuel trim values reveal mixture problems. Cover direct injection vs port injection differences. Link back to the emissions readings from unit 101 — why do they read what they do?
Engine ManagementSensorsFuel TrimLambda
April/May 2026 — Weeks 8–10
Chassis, Geometry and Wheel Alignment
Introduce four-wheel alignment principles: camber, caster, toe, thrust angle. Use the workshop alignment equipment (or a digital gauge and string line if full equipment unavailable) to measure and record geometry on a workshop vehicle. Learners identify and diagnose common geometry faults from tyre wear patterns. Cover front suspension overhaul in full: strut removal and replacement, top mount bearing, spring compressor safety. Extend into rear beam/multi-link suspension identification and inspection.
4-Wheel AlignmentGeometrySuspension OverhaulTyre Wear Diagnosis
May 2026 — Weeks 11–12
Electric and Hybrid Vehicle Technology
Go beyond awareness into technical understanding. Cover HV battery chemistry (NMC, LFP), cell balancing, BMS operation, state of charge vs state of health. Introduce the inverter and motor controller — how DC becomes AC to drive the motor. Regenerative braking — how the system decides blend between friction and regen. Cover charging standards: Type 1/2, CCS, CHAdeMO, rapid vs slow charging rates. Safe isolation procedure in full — PPE, isolation point, discharge verification. This directly prepares learners for L3 EV units.
HV BatteryBMSInverterSafe IsolationL3 Preparation
May/June 2026 — Weeks 13–15
Motorsport Engineering Project
Applied project: learners work in pairs to prepare a workshop vehicle to a set of motorsport-style performance criteria — corner weights within tolerance, tyre pressures to a target, geometry within a specified range, brake balance checked, and all fluids to correct level and condition. Each pair produces a written job card (using motorsport-style documentation) and presents their findings. Optional: introduce data logging using a basic GPS lap timer or accelerometer — what does the data tell you about the vehicle's performance?
Motorsport ProjectCorner WeightsData LoggingJob CardsTeamwork
June 2026 — Week 16
Industry Readiness and L3 Transition
Final week: each learner produces a one-page technical summary of one enrichment topic — aimed at a potential employer or L3 admissions tutor. Peer review session. One-to-one progression meeting: review L2 achievement, map to L3 unit content (which enrichment topics they've already covered), discuss apprenticeship routes and employer expectations. Industry employer or apprenticeship provider visit if achievable. Give learners a skills checklist to take with them — evidence of what they can do beyond the certificate.
